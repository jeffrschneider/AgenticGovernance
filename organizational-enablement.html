Section III, cross-cutting discipline

Organizational enablement

Governance is carried out by people. Organizational enablement sets up the bodies that make decisions, gives every agent a person who answers for it, makes roles clear, and trains the people who sponsor, build and use agents.

What organizational enablement covers

Governance bodies

A governance council sets direction and decides Tier 4 matters. An architecture board reviews designs, and an ethics committee reviews agents whose outcomes affect people.

Roles and accountability

Every agent has a named owner, and every governance activity has someone who decides, someone who acts and people who are informed. Many organizations name a senior officer responsible for AI.

Training and change

Executives, builders and users are trained for what governance asks of them. New governance steps are piloted and explained so that people adopt them instead of working around them.

The accountable people described here are what turns a managed network into a governed one. They set the policy that management enforces, and they read the audit results that Assurance produces.

Control objectives

There are 10 control objectives here. Each one states what it asks for, the risk it addresses and how it is measured. It then says what each risk tier requires, what evidence proves it, and who may check that evidence. The Operating Model page explains the tiers.

ORG-01

Governance bodies that decide

Set up governance bodies, such as a council, an architecture board and an ethics committee, that make timely decisions and see that they are carried out.

Risk addressed
Decisions not made or not enforced
Key metric
Time from request to decision

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Experiments need no body's decision.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Tier 2 decisions are delegated to named reviewers.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Tier 3 decisions go to the architecture board or the ethics committee within a set time.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Tier 4 decisions go to the governance council, with the executive sponsor present.
Evidence
The bodies' charters, their minutes and decision times.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
ORG-02

A named owner for every agent

Give every agent a named person or organization that answers for it, with clear responsibilities.

Risk addressed
Gaps or overlaps in responsibility
Key metric
Share of agents with a named owner

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder owns the experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    A named owner is recorded in the catalog before release.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    The owner has a named deputy, and ownership is confirmed at each review date.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Ownership sits with a senior manager who reports on the agent to the governance council.
Evidence
The owner and deputy in the catalog, and the confirmations at review.
Who may check it
Internal audit, an outside auditor, and a partner.
ORG-03

Who decides, acts and is informed

Write down, for each governance activity, who decides, who acts, who is consulted and who is informed.

Risk addressed
Confusion over who decides
Key metric
Share of governance activities with assigned roles

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder decides and acts for the experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Roles are written down for release and incident decisions.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Roles are written down for every activity on this site that applies to the agent.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Roles are reviewed each year by the governance council.
Evidence
The responsibility matrix and its review date.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-04

Executive training

Train executives in agent governance, so that their decisions on strategy rest on understanding.

Risk addressed
Leaders deciding without understanding
Key metric
Share of executives trained

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No training is required for an experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Sponsors of Tier 2 agents complete the introduction.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Sponsors of Tier 3 agents complete the full course.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Members of the governance council complete the full course and a yearly refresher.
Evidence
Training records.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-05

Builder training

Train the people who build agents in secure, compliant development practice.

Risk addressed
Insecure or non-compliant building
Key metric
Share of builders trained

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Builders read the sandbox rules.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Builders complete the development standards course.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Builders of Tier 3 agents complete the security course.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Builders of Tier 4 agents are certified and refresh their training each year.
Evidence
Training and certification records.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-06

User training

Train the people who use agents in proper use, so that agents are neither misused nor wasted.

Risk addressed
Misuse or little use of agents
Key metric
Share of users trained

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No training is required for an experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Users get a short guide before first use.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Users of Tier 3 agents complete training on what the agent may and may not do.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Users who approve acts for a Tier 4 agent complete training on what they are approving.
Evidence
Training records and the guides issued.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-07

Liaisons in the business

Keep liaisons between governance and each business unit, so that governance stays in step with the work.

Risk addressed
Business units out of touch with governance
Key metric
Business unit satisfaction score

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No liaison is required for an experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Each business unit with production agents has a named liaison.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Liaisons meet governance each month.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Liaisons report on their unit's agents to the governance council.
Evidence
The list of liaisons and meeting records.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-08

Change management

Manage the change governance brings to people's work, so that it is adopted rather than worked around.

Risk addressed
Governance that people avoid
Key metric
Governance adoption rate

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No requirement applies to an experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    New governance steps are announced with guidance.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    New governance steps are piloted and adjusted before they become mandatory.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Adoption is measured and reported to the governance council.
Evidence
Change plans and adoption measurements.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-09

Communication

Tell the people affected what governance requires of them, in plain language.

Risk addressed
People who do not know the rules
Key metric
Awareness survey score

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The sandbox rules are published.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Governance requirements are published where builders and owners work.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Changes to requirements are sent to affected owners before they take effect.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Awareness is surveyed each year.
Evidence
Published requirements and survey results.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
ORG-10

Feedback on governance

Collect feedback on governance itself from the people it affects, and act on it.

Risk addressed
Governance that does not improve
Key metric
Feedback received and acted on

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Builders can send feedback on the sandbox rules.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Feedback is collected after each review.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Feedback is tracked to an action or a reason for no action.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Feedback trends feed the yearly framework review in Continuous improvement.
Evidence
The feedback log and actions taken.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
