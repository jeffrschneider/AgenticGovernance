Section II, lifecycle phase 5 of 6

Change time

Change time governs what happens to an agent after it is running: new versions, new settings and going back to an earlier version. A change that alters what the agent does, refuses, reaches or may spend goes back to Design and Build, which is what makes the lifecycle a loop.

In the Agentic Development Lifecycle, this phase governs the Change stages: Update (stage 21), Reconfigure (stage 22) and Rollback (stage 23). It also draws on Impact (stage 3) before any change is released.

The three kinds of change

Update

An update is a new version with the same identity. It is released as a candidate first, proven against its tests and evaluations, and only then promoted to replace the running version.

Reconfigure

A reconfiguration changes settings, credentials, ceilings or permissions while the version stays the same. It is recorded and approved like a new version, because it can change what the agent is able to do.

Rollback

A rollback returns to an earlier version as a recorded and practised step. Moving the code back is the easy part. Each release also says how the effects of a bad version would be undone.

How change makes the lifecycle a loop

Most changes are routine. A fix that keeps the agent within its declaration goes through Build and is promoted under the controls on this page. A change that alters the agent's declaration, its reach, its limits or the party it may act for is different: in effect it is a new agent arriving under the old name. That kind of change goes back through Design and Build, with a new security review, and the largest changes go back to Plan so that the agent's objective and obligations are confirmed again.

Change feeds back into Design and Build until the agent is decommissioned.

What is learned at Run, such as incidents, evaluation results, cost findings and feedback from partners, is what prompts most changes. Continuous improvement is the home of this loop: it makes sure each finding leads to a change, a new control, or a recorded decision to leave things as they are.

Changes and the agents that depend on them

A change that is safe for its owner can still break the agents and people that rely on it. Before any change is released, everything that depends on the agent is named from the relationship register kept under the Network discipline, and each dependent's owner is told. Dependents outside the organization are told under the notice terms of their contracts.

Control objectives

There are 8 control objectives here. Each one states what it asks for, the risk it addresses and how it is measured. It then says what each risk tier requires, what evidence proves it, and who may check that evidence. The Operating Model page explains the tiers.

CHG-01

Versions on record

Give every release a version, keep the agent's identity across versions, and keep the old versions on record so that anyone can see what ran when.

Risk addressed
Nobody can say which version did what
Key metric
Share of running agents whose version is on record

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The experiment notes its version when it changes.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Every release has a version number and a fingerprint.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Every past version stays retrievable for the retention period, with its build record.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    The version history is part of the evidence an outside auditor can read.
Evidence
The version history with fingerprints, dates and build records.
Who may check it
Internal audit, an outside auditor, and a partner.
CHG-02

Impact on dependents

Before a change, name everything that depends on the agent and answer for each one. A change that is safe for its owner can still break the agents and people that rely on it.

Risk addressed
A change that breaks other teams' work
Key metric
Share of changes with a completed impact analysis

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder checks that no one else uses the experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    The change lists the dependents recorded in the catalog.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Each dependent's owner is told before release and may object, and the analysis is recorded.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Dependents outside the organization are told under the terms of their contracts, with the notice those contracts require.
Evidence
The impact analysis listing each dependent, the notice given and any objection.
Who may check it
Internal audit and a partner.
CHG-03

Approved with a reason

Give each change a written reason and an approver, so that every change to a running agent can be explained later.

Risk addressed
Changes that nobody can explain
Key metric
Share of changes with a reason and an approver

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder writes a one-line reason.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Each change has a reason and is approved by the agent's owner.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Changes are approved by someone other than the person who made them.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Changes that alter what the agent may do or reach are approved by the governance council.
Evidence
The change record with its reason, approver and date.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
CHG-04

Updates proven before promotion

Release a new version as a candidate first, prove it against its tests and evaluations, and only then promote it to replace the running version.

Risk addressed
A new version that is worse than the old one
Key metric
Share of updates promoted after a passed candidate run

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The experiment may be replaced directly.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    A new version passes its tests before it replaces the running one.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    The candidate runs beside the current version, and evaluation results are compared before promotion.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Promotion is staged, and an automatic return to the previous version is ready if quality falls.
Evidence
The candidate's test and evaluation results, compared with the current version's, and the promotion record.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
CHG-05

Reconfiguration as a change

Treat new settings, credentials, ceilings and permissions as changes with the same record as a new version, even when the code stays the same.

Risk addressed
Settings changed without anyone knowing
Key metric
Share of configuration changes with a change record

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Configuration changes are noted by the builder.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Each configuration change is recorded with who made it.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Changes to limits, credentials and permissions need approval before they take effect.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Configuration is held as code, and changes go through the same pipeline as releases.
Evidence
The configuration history with who changed what, when and with whose approval.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
CHG-06

Rollback on purpose

Make going back a version a recorded step that has been practised, and include a plan for undoing what the bad version did, since moving the code back does not undo its effects.

Risk addressed
A failed change with no way back
Key metric
Rollback test success rate

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The experiment can be replaced by its previous copy.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Every release can be rolled back, and rollbacks are recorded.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Rollback is tested before release, and the release names how its effects would be undone.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Rollback and the undoing of effects are rehearsed each quarter.
Evidence
Rollback records and test results, and the plan for undoing effects in each release.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
CHG-07

Changes that go back to design

Send any change that alters what an agent does, refuses, reaches or may spend back through Design and Build, rather than letting it through as a routine update. This is how the lifecycle becomes a loop.

Risk addressed
A new agent arriving under the name of an old one
Key metric
Share of scope changes that went back through design review

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    An experiment that changes its purpose is registered again.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    A change to the agent's declaration needs a design review.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    A change to the agent's declaration, reach or limits goes back through Design and Build with a new security review.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Such a change also returns to Plan, so that its objective and obligations are confirmed again.
Evidence
The comparison of the old and new declarations, and the design review it triggered.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
CHG-08

Emergency changes

Allow an urgent change to skip steps only through a named emergency path, and review it within a set time afterwards.

Risk addressed
Emergencies used to avoid review
Key metric
Emergency changes reviewed within the set time

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No emergency path is needed in a sandbox.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Emergency changes are marked as such and reviewed within a week.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Emergency changes need a named approver at the time and a full review within two working days.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Emergency changes are reported to the governance council, and a pattern of them triggers a review of the agent.
Evidence
The emergency change log, with the approver at the time and the later review.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
