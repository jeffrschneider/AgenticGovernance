Section II, lifecycle phase 6 of 6

Decommission time

Decommission time ends an agent without breaking what depends on it. Dependents are told in time, the agent's data is handled as its declaration says, its records are kept, and its key and its name are retired so that nothing can speak as it afterwards.

In the Agentic Development Lifecycle, this phase governs Decommission (stage 24), and Retract (stage 16) for anything the agent published.

What decommission time covers

Retirement planning

Before shutdown, the organization assesses who and what will be affected, migrates or archives the data according to retention rules, and writes down what was learned from running the agent.

Graceful degradation

Every dependent is told with enough notice to plan, fallback paths are tested, and the agent's address stops answering rather than answering wrongly.

Identity and records

The agent's credentials are revoked and its key is retired. Its name is never given to another agent, and its audit records stay readable for as long as the retention rules require.

An agent usually reaches this phase because its review date found no objective still worth its cost, as section I requires, or because its retirement conditions, set at Plan time, were met.

Control objectives

There are 7 control objectives here. Each one states what it asks for, the risk it addresses and how it is measured. It then says what each risk tier requires, what evidence proves it, and who may check that evidence. The Operating Model page explains the tiers.

DCM-01

Impact assessment

Assess who and what will be affected before an agent is retired, including the systems, people, processes and other agents that rely on it.

Risk addressed
Disruption when an agent is retired
Key metric
Share of retirements with an impact assessment

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder checks that no one else uses the experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    The assessment lists dependents from the catalog.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Each dependent's owner confirms their plan before retirement.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    The executive sponsor approves the retirement after reviewing the assessment.
Evidence
The impact assessment and the confirmations from dependents' owners.
Who may check it
Internal audit and a partner.
DCM-02

Data handled as stated

Migrate, archive or delete the agent's data as its declaration and the retention rules say, before it is shut down.

Risk addressed
Lost data or data kept longer than allowed
Key metric
Share of retirements whose data was handled as declared

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Sandbox data is deleted.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Data is migrated or archived according to the retention rules.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Deletion is verified, and the verification is recorded.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Data handling is certified by the data owner and audited.
Evidence
The record of what data was migrated, archived or deleted, with verification.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
DCM-03

Knowledge transfer

Write down what was learned from running the agent before it is retired, so that the organization keeps it.

Risk addressed
Losing what the organization learned
Key metric
Share of retirements with a knowledge transfer record

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder notes what the experiment showed.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    The owner writes a short retirement note.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    The note covers incidents, limits that were hit and what replaced the agent.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    The note is reviewed by the team that takes over the work.
Evidence
The retirement note in the agent's record.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
DCM-04

Dependents told in time

Tell every dependent system, agent and person that the agent will be retired, early enough for them to plan.

Risk addressed
Dependent systems failing
Key metric
Share of dependents told before the notice period

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No notice is required for an experiment no one else uses.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Dependents in the catalog are told before retirement.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Notice follows a set period, and each dependent acknowledges it.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Partners outside the organization are told under their contracts' notice terms.
Evidence
The notices sent, the dates and the acknowledgements.
Who may check it
Internal audit and a partner.
DCM-05

Records preserved

Keep the audit records, logs and evidence of a retired agent for as long as retention rules require, readable without the agent.

Risk addressed
Being unable to investigate past events
Key metric
Share of retirements with preserved records

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Sandbox logs are kept until the experiment's review is done.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Records are kept for the retention period.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Records stay readable without the retired agent's systems, and retrieval is tested.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    An outside auditor can retrieve the records for the full regulatory period.
Evidence
The archive location and the result of the retrieval test.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
DCM-06

Graceful degradation

Test that dependents handle the agent's absence through fallback paths before it is switched off, and make the agent's address stop answering rather than answer wrongly.

Risk addressed
Hard failures in dependent systems
Key metric
Fallback test success rate

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No requirement applies to an experiment no one else uses.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Dependents confirm they have a fallback.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Fallbacks are tested before shutdown.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    The agent is withdrawn in stages, with a return path until fallbacks are proven.
Evidence
Fallback test results and the shutdown record.
Who may check it
Internal audit and a partner.
DCM-07

Key retired and name never reused

Revoke the agent's credentials and retire its key when it ends, and never give its name to another agent, so that nothing can speak as it afterwards.

Risk addressed
A retired agent's identity used by something else
Key metric
Share of retired agents with revoked credentials and a reserved name

What each tier requires

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Sandbox credentials are revoked.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    All credentials are revoked on retirement.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    The key is retired, and the name is reserved so that it cannot be issued again.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Partners that trusted the agent are told its identity is retired.
Evidence
The revocation record, and the name registry entry marked as retired.
Who may check it
Internal audit, an outside auditor and a partner.
