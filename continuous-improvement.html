Section III, cross-cutting discipline

Continuous improvement

Governance has to change as technology, threats and the business change. This discipline is the home of the lifecycle's loop: it makes sure that what is learned while agents run reaches the people who design and build the next version, and that governance itself keeps improving.

The loop from Change back to Design

Agents are not finished when they are deployed. Incidents, evaluation results, cost findings and feedback from partners all come out of Run time. Change time acts on them, and any change that alters what an agent does, reaches or may spend goes back through Design and Build.

1PlanObjectives confirmed again 2DesignScope reviewed again 3BuildNew version built and proven 4RunFindings recorded 5ChangeFindings acted on 6DecommissionThe loop ends
Every finding leads to a change, a new control, or a recorded decision to leave things as they are.

The rule for this discipline is that no finding is left without an answer. Each one is linked to the design or build change it caused, to a new control, or to a recorded decision not to change anything, with the reason for it.

Improving governance itself

Health and maturity

The organization watches measures that show when its controls are failing, and assesses the maturity of its governance each year, with an outside party at the higher tiers.

Lessons learned

Each incident and audit finding leads to a lesson, and each lesson leads to a change in a standard, a test or a control.

Keeping the framework current

The organization's framework is reviewed each year and after any serious incident, and every revision records what changed and why.

Governance as code

Controls are written as code wherever they can be, so they apply the same way every time and produce their own evidence. Policy written as code is versioned and reviewed like any other change.

Control objectives

There are 7 control objectives here. Each one states what it asks for, the risk it addresses and how it is measured. It then says what each risk tier requires, what evidence proves it, and who may check that evidence. The Operating Model page explains the tiers.

IMP-01

Governance health

Watch the health of governance itself, through measures that show when controls are failing.

Risk addressed
Governance failures nobody notices
Key metric
Share of governance measures reported on time

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The sandbox is reviewed each quarter.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Measures for each discipline are reported each quarter.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Measures are reported each month, with thresholds that trigger review.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Measures are reported to the governance council each month.
Evidence
The governance measures and their history.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
IMP-02

Maturity assessment

Assess the maturity of governance regularly, to find gaps and improve.

Risk addressed
Governance that stops improving
Key metric
Assessments completed on schedule

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No assessment is required for the sandbox alone.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Maturity is assessed each year.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Maturity is assessed each year by someone outside the governance team.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Maturity is assessed each year by an outside party.
Evidence
The assessment reports and the actions taken.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
IMP-03

Comparison with others

Compare governance practice with published practice elsewhere, to find improvements.

Risk addressed
Falling behind good practice
Key metric
Gaps found and closed

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    No comparison is required.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Practice is compared with published guidance each year.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Practice is compared with peers each year.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    The comparison is reported to the governance council with a plan.
Evidence
The comparison and the plan that followed.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
IMP-04

The framework kept current

Revise the organization's framework when technology, threats or the business change, and record what changed and why.

Risk addressed
Governance that does not adapt
Key metric
Time since the last framework revision

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The sandbox rules are revised when needed.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    The framework is reviewed each year.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    The framework is reviewed each year and after any serious incident.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Framework revisions are approved by the governance council.
Evidence
The framework's version history with reasons.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
IMP-05

Lessons learned

Capture lessons from incidents and audits, and feed them back into Design and Build, so that mistakes are not repeated.

Risk addressed
Repeated mistakes
Key metric
Share of incidents with a lesson captured and acted on

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder notes what went wrong.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Each incident has a written lesson.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Each lesson leads to a change in a standard, a test or a control, or a recorded reason for none.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Lessons are shared across business units and reviewed by the governance council.
Evidence
The lessons log and the changes it led to.
Who may check it
Internal audit.
IMP-06

Governance as code

Turn controls into code where they can be, so that they are applied the same way every time and produce their own evidence.

Risk addressed
Manual governance that slows work and varies
Key metric
Share of controls enforced and evidenced by code

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    Sandbox limits are enforced by the platform.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Release gates are automated.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Run-time limits on identity, reach, spend and stopping are enforced by code outside the agent.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    Policies are written as code, versioned and reviewed like any other change.
Evidence
The list of controls with how each is enforced, and the policy code's history.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
IMP-07

The loop from Change back to Design

Make sure what is learned at Run and Change reaches Design and Build: incidents, evaluation results, cost findings and partner feedback each lead to a change, a new control or a recorded decision to leave things as they are.

Risk addressed
The same problems built into the next version
Key metric
Share of run-time findings that reached a design or build decision

What each tier requires (the tiers)

  1. Tier 1 Exploratory
    The builder carries findings into the next experiment.
  2. Tier 2 Operational
    Findings are recorded against the agent and reviewed at the next release.
  3. Tier 3 Critical
    Each finding is linked to the design or build change it caused, or to a decision not to change.
  4. Tier 4 Strategic
    The loop is reviewed each quarter by the governance council across all Tier 4 agents.
Evidence
Findings linked to the changes or decisions that followed them.
Who may check it
Internal audit and an outside auditor.
